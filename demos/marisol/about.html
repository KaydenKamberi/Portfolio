About

Marisol started with a cooler, a station wagon, and a permit problem.

How it started

In 2014 Elena Sotomayor was buying rockfish and shrimp off the boats at Tuna Harbor and selling ceviche out of the back of a station wagon on Prospect Street, mostly to people walking up from the cove. The permits were a problem. By the spring of 2016 she had a lease on a narrow room in La Jolla with a marble counter, six stools, and a wood grill that was slightly too big for the space.

The grill is still slightly too big. The menu has not changed much either: whole fish over fire, tacos, and enough tequila to make a Tuesday feel like something. North Park opened in 2019. Coronado opened in 2022, in a building that had been a bait shop for forty years.

We still buy from the same three boats. When a species is not running it comes off the board for a while, which is why the fish is written in chalk and everything else is printed.

Elena Sotomayor

Chef and owner

Elena grew up in Ensenada, about ninety minutes south of the La Jolla dining room. She learned to break down a fish before she learned to drive, then spent six years cooking in San Francisco — two of them on the grill station at a Basque restaurant where, she says, she learned more about fire than about food. She came back down the coast in 2013 and has not left.

She is behind one of the three counters most nights. If you ask her what to order she will tell you the truth, and it is usually the branzino.

What we cook on

Red oak, with a little mesquite when we can get it. The grill is lit at nine in the morning and burns down all day, so what the fish sits over at seven at night is coals rather than flame. It is the reason the kitchen is hot and the reason the branzino tastes the way it does.
