# Case Studies

## marisol

**Title:** Marisol &mdash; Coastal Kitchen & Bar

**One-line result:** A wood-fired Baja seafood brand with three San Diego counters that feels like a sun-bleached postcard

**The brief:** Marisol needed a site that matched the warmth and craft of its food: handwritten chalkboards, turquoise tiles, and the smell of wood smoke. The owner wanted the online menu to feel like the paper one left on each table, the locations page to double as a reservation hub, and the whole experience to load fast on the slow cafe Wi-Fi.

**What I built:** A four-page responsive site that leans on Tailwind for layout and a custom CSS layer for the brand's handmade feel. The hero pairs a big serif headline with a full-bleed photo of the La Jolla deck; the menu page groups dishes into visual grids with prices in the brand's coral; locations are full-screen cards with photos of each space and direct "Reserve" CTAs; and the about page tells the origin story over a wood-fire photo. The header shrinks on scroll, and the whole site weighs under 1.2 MB.

**Key features:**
- Scrolling menu with grouped categories and price display
- Location pages with integrated reservation system
- Responsive design that works on phones and tablets

**Tags:** restaurant, food & beverage, multi-location, reservation system, Tailwind CSS

## construction

**Title:** Dad's Hire-Me Site

**One-line result:** A bold, no-nonsense lead generator for a handyman that converts calls on a $0 budget

**The brief:** A local handyman needed a simple, fast-loading website to showcase his work and generate phone calls. The site had to be easy to navigate, clearly display his services and service area, and work well on mobile devices for customers searching on their phones. No complex features, just a straightforward presentation of what he does and how to contact him.

**What I built:** A four-page static site with a clean, bold design that puts the phone number front and center. The home page features a hero with a clear value proposition, a scrolling testimonial marquee, and photo tiles of recent work. Services are listed with checkmarks, and the work page shows before/after photo pairs. All photos are self-hosted WebP under 200 KB, and the site uses self-hosted fonts to eliminate third-party requests. A sticky call bar at the bottom ensures the phone number is always accessible.

**Key features:**
- Mobile-first responsive design with large tap targets
- Photo gallery with before/after comparisons
- Scrolling testimonial marquee for social proof

**Tags:** handyman, trades, lead generation, mobile-first, static site

## tattoo

**Title:** Ink & Echo Tattoo Collective

**One-line result:** A dark, bold portfolio site for a Portland tattoo collective that showcases artistry and drives bookings

**The brief:** Ink & Echo needed a website that reflected their edgy, artistic brand while making it easy for potential clients to browse styles, view artist portfolios, and book consultations. The design had to be distinctly different from the other demos in the portfolio, with a dark color scheme that lets the tattoo artwork pop. The site needed to handle image-heavy content efficiently and provide clear paths to booking for each artist.

**What I built:** A complete concept website featuring a home page with hero section, artist profiles, featured work gallery, tattoo styles showcase, booking process explanation, FAQ section, and location/hours information. A dedicated gallery page uses a masonry layout with filterable categories. The design uses a dark background with electric purple and cyan accents, custom typography with Bebas Neue for headlines and Inter for body text. All pages include the concept banner and maintain consistent branding.

**Key features:**
- Responsive masonry gallery with style filtering
- Interactive FAQ accordion
- Artist profile cards with specialty tags

**Tags:** tattoo, creative, portfolio, gallery, dark theme
